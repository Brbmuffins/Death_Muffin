# Death Muffin lore: the story (working draft)

The team's working draft of the game's story: worked out by Helix with Claude on 2026-10-27 from War's "Genesis to Present Day" doc
and the #lore post "Current Lore", and posted in #lore ("Genesis to Preset Day - Reworked Lore Take 1") for the team to review.
Nothing here is settled until the team says so, and none of it is in the game yet: what players read today is in
`client/data/content/` (`codex.json`, `bosses.json`, `areas.json`). The writing rules and the rest of the lore bible are a ROADMAP item
("the writing rules and the lore bible").

*Akar couldn't let his father go, so now nobody can let go of anyone.*

## 1. The Exiled Gods
Long ago, thirteen gods were cast out of Heaven and sent down to Earth, where people came to call them the Titans. Anu, the first of them, led the others, with his wife Eath beside him. They kept their immortality, but they could not leave.

The Titans had children, the Firstborne, who lived far longer than any person today but could still die. Among them was Akar, the son of Anu and Eath. Each generation after the Firstborne was a little less divine than the one before, until the line ran down to ordinary people.

When any of the gods' children died, their soul went home to the nearest god, the way a child runs to a parent. For a thousand years that was enough. The Titans and their children lived in peace and built great cities.

## 2. The Way Home
Then the Titans found a way back to Heaven: the Sacraments, a death rite in seven stages.
1. Digging the grave
2. Laying the bones
3. Singing the hymn
4. Washing and anointing
5. Burning
6. Returning to earth and water, and mourning
7. The Tolling of the Bell

Each stage stripped away part of a Titan's immortality. The last one, the bell, was rung as the Titan died, and it carried the soul up to Heaven.

Seven Firstborne were chosen to keep the rite, one for each stage. These were the Sacramenters. Each swore a vow to see every body through their stage, and in return each received a fragment of divine immortality and a life longer than any other Firstborne.

## 3. The Promise
Word spread that once the gods were home, they would raise their children up after them, and people rejoiced.

The Covenant was founded on that promise. It gave the rite to everyone, so every burial ended with the great bell, rung to celebrate a soul passing on. The bell hung in the grand cathedral above Hollowmere, and you could hear it for miles.

It mattered more than anyone knew. A soul could find a god on Earth by itself, but Heaven was too far without help. As the gods left, the bell became the only way home.

## 4. Why the Gods Left
Nobody knows why the gods really left.

Some went to save their children, meaning to reach Heaven and raise them up. Some just wanted to go home, because Earth had always been a punishment. Some may have been running from something. And some promised to come back without knowing if they could. Two thousand years later, every faith has its own favourite gods to quote, and all of them have real words to point to.

In Year 1000, Cartha became the first to complete the Sacraments. Her soul left her body in a blinding light and seemed to rise, and most people took it as proof. Over the next 553 years, the rest followed.

Garrel was one of them. He was never meant to be exiled, so he was the one god Heaven had wronged, and the one it might listen to. He went up on purpose, to argue for the children left behind. Before he went, he gave Akar a plain glass orb and said: *"If you hear nothing from me, keep them here."*

No word ever came, not from Garrel and not from any of them.

## 5. The Son
Akar watched the gods leave one by one.

His father stayed until the very end. Anu was the first of the gods and their leader, and he saw every one of them home before he would go himself. But he never told his son why he was leaving.

By then Akar was old even for a Firstborne. For centuries he had waited for a sign and heard nothing, and his fear kept growing. Maybe Heaven had turned the children away. Maybe none of the gods had made it at all, and the bell led nowhere.

## 6. The Breaking (Year 1553)
When Anu lay dying, Akar snapped.

He meant to save the sons and daughters of the gods. If the gods had abandoned them, his father was the last divine thing left on Earth, and the only thing that could keep their souls safe. And he could not let his father go without knowing why.

So he cursed the bell.

The Prelate, keeper of the seventh stage, rang it as Anu died. The bell broke as it tolled, and the Prelate was fused to it. In that moment, Akar caught his father's soul in Garrel's glass orb.

He set the orb into a staff of his own making, a wicked-looking thing: the Soul Prison. As long as he holds it, his father's divinity keeps him alive, and he has not let go of it since. He disappeared into the dark beneath the cathedral.

## 7. The Cost
He aimed at one soul and caught them all.

Every soul goes to the nearest god, and the only god left on Earth was Anu, inside the staff. So every soul that dies is pulled into the Soul Prison. Akar was never meant to hold so many. The prison overflows, the line between life and death wears thin, and souls leak back into the world as the restless dead.

People bury their mothers and fathers, their sons and daughters, and they come back.

The Covenant has cast bell after bell. Every new bell rings and nothing happens, because the curse was on the rite, not the metal. The churchyard is full of silent bells.

It's worst in Hollowmere, where the bell hung and where the curse began.

## 8. The Seven Keepers
The curse bound the seven Sacramenters to their vows. The rite could never finish, and they could never stop, and their divine fragments kept them alive. For two thousand years they kept doing their duty, until the death and evil around them consumed them and the vow was all that was left.

They became monstrosities of what they swore. They can no longer tell the living from the dead, so anything that enters their stage is one more body to see through.
- **The Gravedigger King**, keeper of the grave, in the Hollow Graves
- **The Bone Abbess**, keeper of the bones, in the Marrow Ossuary
- **The Drowned Congregation**, keepers of the hymn, in the Drowned Nave
- **The Plague Saint**, keeper of the washing and anointing, in the Plague Cloister
- **The Cinder Regent**, keeper of the burning, in the Cinder Pyre
- **The Mire Mother**, keeper of the earth, water and mourning, in the Mourning Fen
- **The Bell-Sworn Prelate**, keeper of the bell, in the Bell Sanctum

To call a keeper, you offer soul shards. You are holding a funeral, and the keeper comes to perform its stage, because its vow allows nothing else.

Their stages lie between the world and the depths where Akar hides, and in two thousand years nobody has got past all seven. Apart from Akar, they are the last of the Firstborne still alive. Every other child of the gods is long dead. Those who died after the curse walk with the restless dead, stronger than most.

## 9. What the World Remembers
Everyone knows that Akar stole his father's soul as Anu died. Most believe the bell broke because the rite was interrupted, and that Akar himself died long ago.

Nobody knows he cursed the bell on purpose. Nobody knows that every soul is pulled into his staff, or that this is why the dead rise.

Only his Followers insist he's still alive down there, holding on. The Faithful call that a fairy tale.

## 10. The Faiths and the Classes
**The Faithful (the Covenant).** They believe the gods reached Heaven and their children are meant to follow, and they hate Akar for stealing Anu's ascension. For two thousand years they have tried to keep the dead down, and they keep failing.
- **Grave Warden:** a Covenant member who guards the graves. The Warden's chain binds the dead back into the ground.

**The Followers of Akar (the necromancers: Ossuary, Gravecaller, Mourner, Rotweaver).** They believe Akar was right to keep a god on Earth, and that without him the souls of ordinary people would be lost forever. They use the trapped dead, and they want the staff passed to a stronger hand before Akar fails. Their power only exists because of the curse: souls can't leave, so the dead are there to command. They honour the man who ruined the world, and what they do with the dead is what slowly consumed the keepers.

**Bell Monk.** He keeps the same faith as the Covenant, but he left the church to practise it alone and won't say why. He knew something the others didn't, and he kept quiet. He wears a brown hooded robe with a wooden cross on a rope, and fights with holy water and prayers.

**Hollow Knight.** A soldier who swore to protect the living from the undead. When he died, his vow kept his soul from resting, so he rose to keep fighting. His vow kept him himself, while the keepers' vows consumed them. He doesn't trust the Followers, but they fight the same enemy.

**Carrion Witch.** She belongs to no faith and thinks the dead are the Covenant's job. But the undead are killing her animals, poisoning the water and trampling the herbs and mushrooms she uses. So she and her crows, rats and spiders fight back, fed up that the Covenant can't do its job.

**Veilwalker and Reaper** are on the shelf for now.

## 11. The Player's Road
You begin in Hollowmere as one of these, with your own reason. Your road runs through the seven keepers, toward the grand cathedral, and down.

Beating a keeper finishes its stage for the souls trapped there, and they move on to the next stage. That part of the world goes quiet. Beat all seven, and every soul is waiting at the broken bell for a toll that can't come.

Along the way, the keepers' clues show that the bell didn't simply break. Someone cursed it, and that is why the dead rise.

Then you go into the Catacomb Depths: *Down, and further down.*

## 12. Akar
At the bottom, Akar is still alive and still holding the staff.

He isn't fighting to kill you. He's fighting not to let go, because to him you're the one about to doom every soul he's protecting. As he takes damage, the crystal cracks, souls pour out, and his father's light shows through. The more you win, the more the prison leaks.

He says almost nothing, except perhaps one question: did Garrel ever send word?

## Never answered, on purpose
- Why the gods really left, and whether any of them reached Heaven.
- Why Anu was leaving.
- Whether Anu is aware inside the staff, and whether he knew what his son would do.
- What happens to a soul released from the staff.
- Where Garrel's orb came from.
- Why the Monk left the church.
- Whether the Prelate knew the bell was cursed.

## Ideas still floating (not agreed yet)
- **The ending:** break the staff and release every soul to who knows where, or take it and become the next Akar. The choice could depend on your class.
- **You can't stay dead either:** no soul can pass on, so the player comes back after dying. The curse becomes the reason for runs and Ascension.
- **Eath's part:** she left first without saying goodbye, or she never left at all.
- **The orb came from Heaven with Garrel:** which would mean Heaven handed Akar the tool.
- **Later layout:** Hollowmere as the starting zone, with the grand cathedral as the destination.
- **Section 9** (what the world knows about Akar) is one of four options and isn't picked yet.
